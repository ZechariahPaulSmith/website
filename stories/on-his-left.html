ON HIS LEFT.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Laying on his left side was more comfortable. In this way his stomach relaxed, making him less nauseous: a feeling common to when he rested on his right. This improvement however came at the cost of facing the window where his thin curtains did nothing but tint the sunlight that burst into the room each morning to interrupt his sleep long before he had any intention of waking up. Even now he remains in bed while the sun slowly rises, eyes closed, wishing in vain he was still asleep. Nausea or the sun was an ever present dilemma in his thoughts and every night a new winner was crowned as he decided one kind of suffering was preferable to the other.

People often recommended remedies: "get thicker curtains," "move your bed," or "wear an eye-mask." All things he could do, but against each, over time, he constructed defenses, some more reasonable than others. He could get new curtains, but that was always too much effort in going to the store, selecting new curtains, then replacing and discarding the old ones. On top of this it was only in the morning that the curtains were an issue. For the rest of the day he quite liked the slight dimming of the sunlight when the curtains were closed. His home could remain private and bright. He could also move his bed, and while this, he decided, was less effort than getting new curtains, it was always something to do 'later.' And when he was honest with himself, he quite liked his room the way it was. Of course he had considered wearing an eye-mask, owning several over his time in this apartment; thinking each time that maybe this time it will help, but each time he slides the mask on and pushes through the discomfort, the sun greets his weary eyes as he finds the mask vanished in the night.

Not able to defeat the sun, he decides to tackle the nausea. His doctor might know what to do and writing a note to remind him, he found that everytime he found himself in that sterile white room, the unconcerned face of the physician before him, the note would have slipped from his mind or his person, lost on the way in or forgotten crumpled in his pocket, always remembering or finding the note after he'd left and all he could do was sigh at the passing of another wasted chance.
